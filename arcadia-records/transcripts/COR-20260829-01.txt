This message is a formal written objection to approval of TRH 26-11
and TRE 26-09 on the present record, a request that the City withhold
action until the evidentiary defects identified below are cured, and a
submission for inclusion in the complete administrative record for
both permits and every related environmental, grading,
Olympic-delivery, or appellate proceeding. Attached as Exhibit A is
Zero-Removal First: Santa Anita / LA28, a 21-page administrative
alternatives memorandum issued 29 August 2026. Please preserve this
message with its headers and attachment as received, circulate it to
every City employee, consultant, reviewing official, and legal adviser
participating in the permits, and confirm by 5:00 p.m. on Wednesday,
September 2, 2026 that it has been entered into the project file.

I am past the stage of requesting general assurances that safety,
trees, and community concerns have been considered. The City is
reviewing an application to remove 34 protected oaks, plant 34
replacement trees, and authorize construction or course activity
within the protected zones of 45 additional oaks. Arcadia Municipal
Code section 9110.01.070 requires the healthy-tree record to explain
why removal is necessary and why removal is more desirable than
alternative project designs. The same section requires an equivalent
necessity and alternatives explanation for protected-zone
encroachment. The legal object is therefore specific. The applicant
must demonstrate why this selected geometry defeats reasonably
feasible lower-impact designs under disclosed mandatory criteria.

The public record presently does not permit that determination. As of
29 August 2026, the City project page links to the course map,
arborist assessment, biological resources assessment, precise grading
plan, and rough grading plan, but each of those five links returns a
404 error. The permit remains listed as pending. The public therefore
cannot inspect the selected route, surveyed tree locations and
protected zones, grading, drainage, utilities, construction limits, or
the technical basis for the proposed impacts. A comment or decision
clock should not run against a record that the City itself has made
unavailable. The files should be restored, completeness should be
disclosed, and a meaningful review period should begin only after the
complete technical record is accessible.

The only publicly reported alternatives claim is that an earlier route
affected more trees and was modified. That statement establishes that
the course geometry is adjustable. It does not establish that the
current route is necessary, that it is the least damaging reasonably
feasible route, or that a route designed from the outset around
immutable protected-tree exclusions was ever tested. Exhibit A
supplies a falsifiable study protocol, identified as ZR-1, that
imports the actual survey, grading, drainage, utilities, safety,
welfare, access, and sporting criteria and then attempts to route the
course through Santa Anita's existing tracks, turf, infield arenas,
open event areas, parking hardscape, service corridors, stabling,
veterinary capacity, and temporary-overlay systems without removal.
ZR-1 is not represented as a finished civil design or FEI
certification. Its purpose is to require the applicant to test the
available site capacity rather than treating the preferred course line
as the fixed constraint.

That study is proportionate to the site. The FEI describes Santa Anita
as a 320-acre equestrian facility with a 26,000-seat grandstand, an
infield capable of accommodating 50,000 people, 61 barns for more than
2,000 horses, and an on-course veterinary clinic. Santa Anita has
separately installed major show arenas and schooling infrastructure in
the infield, used the north parking lot for temporary stabling, and
described those investments as part of a strategy to create a
world-class equestrian venue. A property of that scale may still
present genuine Olympic cross-country constraints, but those
constraints must be demonstrated with maps, measurements, standards,
and alternative comparisons. They cannot be presumed from the selected
design.

The permanent residual makes the record more demanding. Santa Anita's
vice president of equestrian operations stated publicly that the
cross-country course will remain after the Games and will permit Santa
Anita to invite additional forms of horse sport. The City is therefore
considering irreversible environmental authorization that would also
expand a privately controlled operating platform after 2028. The
record should identify the complete capital stack; distinguish
temporary overlay, permanent Olympic-triggered work, and private
elective enhancement; state who pays for and owns each retained
improvement; disclose useful life, maintenance, restoration,
scheduling, and commercial rights; and identify any enforceable
public-access consideration received by Arcadia.

I have located no published easement, trail dedication, public-use
covenant, bicycle right, family walking right, or recurring
free-access schedule attached to this permanent course. Santa Anita's
current visitor rules prohibit bicycles inside the park. The City
should answer the practical public-benefit question in writing: after
the Games leave and the course remains, may an Arcadia resident enter
the improved area with children for an ordinary walk, ride a bicycle
through it, or use it without admission to a privately programmed
event? If the answer depends entirely on Santa Anita's continuing
permission, the record should describe the residual accurately as a
privately controlled asset rather than imply a public recreational
right that has not been created.

The replacement proposal also requires an express finding. Arcadia's
default rule is at least two new 24-inch-box trees for every protected
tree approved for removal, subject to the Director's power to modify
that requirement when appropriate. Thirty-four removals begin from a
default of 68 replacement trees, while the project page identifies 34.
The City should disclose whether a reduction has been requested or
proposed, identify the official exercising modification authority, and
publish the written basis, species, locations, establishment
obligations, mortality security, maintenance responsibility, and time
required to approach the canopy and habitat functions lost at removal.

LA28's own public materials describe a no-permanent-build or
no-new-permanent-venues delivery model based on existing venues,
temporary facilities, and temporary overlays. Santa Anita is an
existing venue, but that does not answer how new grading, drainage,
footing, road, utility, landscape, or course work retained after 2028
is classified. The permit record should provide component-level
accounting so that permanent Olympic-triggered work does not disappear
inside the general description of an existing venue.

Exhibit A maps the relevant actors, authority, site capacity, residual
value, Arcadia-wide land-use context, technical study, and records
schedule. It deliberately does not allege corruption, does not depend
on a hidden-development theory, and does not assert a Trammell Crow
connection to these permits without documentary evidence. Those
exclusions are intentional. They leave the City with a direct
administrative question that cannot be answered by disputing motive,
tone, or politics. A responsive answer must identify the document,
criterion, official, analysis, and finding that supports the proposed
action.

Before either permit is decided, please provide one consolidated
written response stating whether the City will keep both permits
pending; the date each application was deemed complete and the
resulting decision deadline; the date on which all five technical
files will be restored; whether the applicant submitted a comparative
alternatives analysis and where it can be reviewed; whether a
zero-removal or tree-exclusion design was tested, by whom, and under
what criteria; the mandatory tree-specific conflict asserted for each
proposed removal and encroachment; the proposed replacement ratio and
findings supporting any reduction from 68; the temporary and permanent
project components, their funding and post-Games ownership; every
binding public-access right created by the project; the
environmental-review status; the identity of the initial
decision-maker; the form and date of formal notice; and the applicable
appeal deadline.

The City can acknowledge this filing by producing a reviewable record
rather than convening another community presentation. If the City
proceeds without a written response, the administrative record will
show that these specific defects were identified while the permits
were pending, that a concrete and technically testable lower-impact
study was supplied, that the City's own principal technical links were
inaccessible, and that no written answer was provided before action.
Any nonresponse will be recorded only as notice without a written
answer, not as agreement.

Please also preserve all responsive records, including superseded
alignments, geospatial files, native plans, tree data, grading and
drainage files, decision logs, safety and welfare criteria, emails and
attachments, meeting records, funding and ownership agreements, legacy
and access terms, replacement-tree analyses, environmental-review
records, and communications with Santa Anita Park, 1/ST, LA28, FEI,
course-design personnel, engineers, arborists, biologists,
contractors, and consultants. A companion California Public Records
Act request is being submitted separately through the City's
designated records channel so that the disclosure track is assigned
its own number and statutory determination.

The controlling question is this: what mandatory, disclosed, and
independently verified technical requirement makes removal of these 34
protected oaks and encroachment upon 45 additional protected zones
more desirable than a tested zero-removal route using Santa Anita's
extensive existing site capacity, and what enforceable public right
does Arcadia receive for the permanent privately controlled residual
its permits would authorize?
