What is being examined

The historical proposal identifies 34 protected-oak removals, encroachment into 45 protected-tree zones and 34 replacement trees. The August notice identifies 33 Coast Live Oaks and one Engelmann Oak among the proposed removals. These are proposed quantities, not a statement that removal was approved. PROD-20261026-002 · p. 1

The August 29 submission asks for a comparative lower-impact design record, tree-specific necessity, mitigation findings and disclosure of permanent works, funding and public-access terms. Its ZR-1 concept is a testable feasibility-study proposal, with conceptual geometry rather than a surveyed or FEI-certified course. The later-produced records now supply actual applications, an alignment graphic and staff memoranda against which those questions can be tested. SUB-001 · p. 1 SUB-001 · p. 4

F01 · City position

The permit review and records request have separate states

Planning’s September 9 message says Santa Anita requested that processing of TRH 26-11 / TRE 26-09 be placed on hold while the FEI course-design team, LA28 and engineers consider tree impacts. It states that a revised application would restart public notification. The October 26 records-production email promises another update on or before November 30, 2026. A production email supplies evidence; it does not decide or restart the permits.

F02 · Observed staff interpretation

The 2025 zoning memo sets out a ministerial construction approach

The September 2025 memorandum classifies the equestrian course as a permitted horseracing-related use and says the described construction would require ministerial permits without discretionary review. It separately flags permanent structures or lighting for possible Site Plan and Design Review. For the RTE overlay, it proposes interpreting the 100-foot equipment/apparatus restriction as applying to permanent installations, with temporary equipment potentially treated differently. It also calls for an Impact Mitigation and Neighborhood Compatibility Plan approved before construction. These are the memo’s positions, not this archive’s legal conclusions or evidence that permits were issued.

F03 · Observed staff planning

The staff work memo connects approvals, agreements and public assets

The work memo describes an initial Venue City Agreement approved by the Council in October and further agreements addressing responsibilities and reimbursement. It says the zoning approach had been circulated internally and agreed as a process. Its marketing section describes LA28 packaging City advertising assets for sponsors, while anticipating Council action if those assets were used beyond existing nonprofit/community-event policies. It discusses pedestrian access from public streets into the venue and possible grant or CDBG support as options. These passages establish subjects being planned and assigned; they do not establish the final contracts, spending, sponsorship awards or binding public-access rights.

F04 · Applicant statements

The applications supply necessity and alternatives answers

Both redacted applications identify Los Angeles Turf Club as applicant and Santa Anita Land Holdings as property owner. Their second pages explain impacts through grading, drainage, access, course geometry, tree condition and claimed limits on further redesign. The removal application requests 1:1 replacement and continuing protection and monitoring of retained trees. The encroachment application specifies fencing, excavation controls, arborist hold points and stop-work triggers. The approval/denial fields on the produced first pages are blank. Accordingly, the record contains answers that must be tested, rather than an absence of any explanation.

F05 · Observed graphic; interpretation bounded

An actual alignment comparison is in the October production

The one-page Course Alignment Comparison overlays red and white lines on an oblique aerial view of the hillside. It supplies graphical comparison evidence beyond the arborist narrative. F06 · Observed arithmetic

The protected-tree accounting contains a two-tree inconsistency

The arborist report divides 92 evaluated trees into 25 standard encroachments, 20 intensive-mitigation encroachments, 34 removal-pathway trees and 13 protection/avoidance trees. Those categories yield 58 retained or avoided trees, as does 92 minus 34. Several narrative passages instead state 56. The report includes tree-specific tables and an alternatives narrative, so the issue is reconciling its internal count and testing its rationale. The inconsistency alone establishes neither misconduct nor a change in the stated 34-removal proposal.

F07 · Applicant request / consultant rationale

The replacement ratio is requested, not shown as adopted here

The application and arborist report explicitly request one replacement for each of the 34 proposed removals. The report gives a preservation-context rationale and proposes establishment, monitoring and failure-replacement measures. The archive distinguishes that request from the City’s acceptance, conditions or modification findings. The historical notice likewise describes a proposal for 34 replacements; it is not a final mitigation decision.

F08 · Consultant conclusion

The ecological conclusion depends on stated conditions

The biological report describes the north hillside as a disturbed coast live oak grove within a developed operational setting, identifies native tree resources, and places protected-tree analysis in Appendix A. Its no-significant-biological-effect conclusion relies on compliance with City requirements, wildlife protections and construction practices. The original is 136 physical PDF pages: the 100-page arborist appendix occupies pages 27–126, followed by occurrence, photographic and species-list appendices. The page offset matters when checking citations. This archive does not substitute document extraction for independent ecological or engineering validation.

How to use this archive

Authority. This is an independently maintained collection by Jonathan Sandhu, not a City website or a final administrative record certified by the City. City statements, applicant submissions, consultant reports and this archive’s analysis retain separate authority. The archive makes no unsupported finding of corruption, hidden development, or a Trammell Crow connection.

Preservation. Original City-produced archives and PDFs retain their bytes and City redactions. Correspondence is published as explicitly labeled transcripts with private contact details, quoted thread history and routing material omitted. Unrelated family correspondence, authentication data and internal automation files remain private. Public HTML captures use a .txt suffix to prevent embedded scripts from executing on this site.

Review depth. All ten ZIP members are inventoried, hash-checked, extracted for native text and rendered for inspection. Narrative records received initial substantive review; maps and scanned application fronts received visual inspection. Drawings, species assessments and tree tables remain source evidence rather than an independent field survey, engineering certification or legal determination. Searchable text may omit image-only details or contain PDF font-encoding errors; the original PDF controls.

Versions. ZIP-20260924-002 and PUB-003 are each 136 pages, including the 100-page arborist appendix at pages 27–126. The initial summary’s shorter file-page count is corrected here. The report byte variants remain separate. View comparison method and results.

Changes. The first private compilation had not retrieved the two ZIPs. This edition closes that retrieval gap and revises the account to incorporate the produced applications, graphical comparison and planning memoranda. Change log · Edition status · Maintenance status.
