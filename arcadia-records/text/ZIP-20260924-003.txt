1. Why is it necessary to encroach upon the tree(s)? 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
2. Why is encroachment upon the tree(s) more desirable than an alternative project design? 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3. What mitigation measures are included in the project to ensure that encroachment will 
not eventually result in the loss of the protected tree(s)? 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 To facilitate the construction of the Cross-Country Course for the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic Games. Encroachment is necessary where the final grading envelope, drainage, access, or course geometry cannot 
fully avoid the protected zone while maintaining functional equestrian-course design. The TRE categories 
identify trees where the encroachment can be managed without removal if protection measures are 
implemented and final work remains within the assumptions used for impact analysis
For retained trees, managed encroachment is preferable to removal and is also preferable to redesign 
that would materially impair course function, safety or shift impacts to other protected trees. TRE 
processing allows the course design to retain trees while applying measurable protection controls, 
monitoring, and construction limitations.
Mitigation for encroachment includes fencing, signage, exclusion of storage and equipment, hand/air 
excavation, arborist hold points, root-pruning limits, mulch and soil moisture management, pruning 
standards, monitoring, and stop-work triggers if roots or conditions exceed assumptions.
