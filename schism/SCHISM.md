# SCHISM

## Darkhive Stack Sovereignty Dossier

Jonathan Sandhu · Public edition 1.2 · 2026-11-26

Original privately circulated 2026-06-26. Public edition first published 2026-11-26.

FleetForge addresses a consequential industrial problem: keeping software maintainable across a growing population of devices. Darkhive's public materials describe secure software delivery, fleet management and adaptable production. SCHISM begins by recognizing the value of that direction, then examines the commercial and institutional authority needed to sustain it through supplier changes. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3) [S7](#s7)

Customer control rests on the rights, records and institutional capacity that survive a supplier change. A delivery platform can distribute an update while its customer remains dependent on other organizations for permission, maintenance or support. SCHISM examines where that dependence sits and what a purchaser would need to establish a credible claim of independence. The June 26, 2026 thesis appears here as a sourced public procurement and governance analysis.

## 01. The board-level thesis

Software delivery creates value; durable customer authority determines its reach.

Darkhive's software direction has a substantial public history. The government's FleetForge Phase I record describes a continuous integration and delivery platform, with an award beginning in November 2022. The September 2024 Series A announcement describes secure software delivery and an intended expansion into comprehensive fleet and device management. That history supports treating FleetForge as an established strategic undertaking. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2)

SCHISM's contribution is an analytical extension: evaluate the platform by the authority it leaves with its customer throughout the asset's life. The relevant decisions include accepting changes, retaining records, arranging support and changing suppliers. A business that coordinates these decisions can reduce integration costs and improve continuity. Its durable advantage depends on making the responsibilities legible and the promised independence demonstrable within a defined scope. Successful integration can concentrate customer reliance on the integrator, which makes the integrator's own continuity arrangements commercially significant.

## 02. Five control surfaces

A common vocabulary for allocating responsibility.

The original dossier organized dependence around compute, boot, communications, update and evidence. This edition retains those categories at the level of ownership and support. Each surface carries a separate decision about access, responsibility or continuity; a broad platform claim can conceal materially different arrangements across them. A customer may possess extensive application rights while depending on another organization for maintenance or the records needed to justify a change.

This is an author-defined procurement taxonomy. It helps distinguish the capability a supplier advertises from the authority a particular customer receives. The useful output is an explicit allocation of responsibilities, together with the limits and duration of each commitment.

| Surface | Governance concern |
| --- | --- |
| Compute | Responsibility for the supported execution environment and its dependencies. |
| Boot and trust | Authority over accepted system changes and continuity of support. |
| Communications | Interface rights, supplier dependencies and documented compatibility boundaries. |
| Update | Maintenance obligations, change approval and recovery responsibility. |
| Evidence | Custody, completeness and continued accessibility of decision records. |

## 03. The cost of dependence

Supplier changes expose obligations that acquisition price leaves unpriced.

A supplier change becomes expensive when several dependencies must change together. Software formats, support agreements, maintenance records and acceptance decisions can bind the customer to a relationship long after the original purchase. The exposure grows when one party controls an indispensable resource and a successor lacks either the right to use it or the records required to understand it. That is the mechanism behind the lock-in concern in the original dossier.

A useful commercial assessment therefore distinguishes purchase price from the cost of preserving choice. It considers support duration, access to records, staff continuity, transition assistance and the work required to restore an accepted state. Concentration can still be an economically rational choice. Its terms should make the exposure visible, including which promises survive a product's retirement, an acquisition of the supplier or the end of a support agreement. A lower initial price may reasonably purchase a narrower support commitment; the decision becomes distorted when the purchaser assumes continuity that the supplier never priced or promised.

## 04. Procurement priorities

Allocate independence to the decisions with the greatest consequence.

Procurement can distinguish the immediate value of a product from the continuity required of a long-lived service. The first concerns what the purchaser receives now. The second concerns the support, records and authority needed over time. Different assets can justify different arrangements, provided the purchasing organization records the resulting dependency and the person responsible for accepting it. Independence also has a carrying cost: documentation, institutional knowledge and transition readiness require continuing investment. The buyer should concentrate that investment where loss of choice would impose the greatest burden.

NIST's Secure Software Development Framework supplies a shared vocabulary for software security and supplier communication during acquisition. SCHISM adds an economic question to that conversation: which responsibilities must remain available if commercial circumstances change? Procurement documents should connect each material requirement to a deliverable, an accountable party and a stated duration. That connection turns an aspiration for independence into something the buyer can evaluate and price. [S9](#s9)

## 05. Authority and recovery

Continuity requires an institution capable of making and owning decisions.

NIST's platform firmware guidance treats protection, detection and recovery as connected aspects of resilience, and explicitly identifies procurement as an application of the guidance. At the governance level, this directs attention toward the parties responsible for maintaining a trustworthy state and restoring service after disruption. [S8](#s8)

Customer authority requires more than the availability of a technical mechanism. An organization needs designated decision owners, suitable records and a support arrangement that remains usable during an interruption. The commercial agreement should identify the responsibilities that persist through personnel changes and supplier withdrawal. A concentration of authority may be justified by security or accountability requirements; its continuity obligations should be correspondingly explicit. SCHISM evaluates that allocation of responsibility without prescribing a particular implementation. The distinction matters because organizational dependence can persist even after technical access has been granted, particularly when only one party understands the maintenance history.

## 06. Evidence for a claim

Match the scope of the conclusion to the scope of the record.

A claim of independence needs a named subject, a defined boundary and evidence of the authority being claimed. A product description establishes the supplier's stated proposition. A contract records commitments between parties. An observed result establishes behavior under the conditions that produced it. Keeping these categories distinct allows a reader to assess the strength of a conclusion without treating every document as interchangeable proof. A funding announcement records the announced financing event and intended resources; it leaves product behavior and customer authority to their respective evidence. An investment thesis may anticipate those outcomes, while verification requires a subsequent record.

The evidence should identify its date, owner, relevant configuration, applicable rights and unresolved dependencies. It should also retain findings that narrow the claim. NIST's SSDF provides a general framework for communicating secure development practices; a reference to that framework supplies context for an assessment. The resulting conclusion still requires evidence tied to the actual product, agreement and customer responsibility under review. [S9](#s9)

## 07. Interfaces and portability

Usable access connects published interfaces to institutional continuity.

Darkhive's 2024 public description emphasizes open architecture and documented interfaces while also describing a proprietary software delivery platform. Those characteristics can coexist: interface access and ownership of the surrounding implementation concern different parts of the commercial relationship. Their practical value depends on the particular rights and documentation supplied to the customer. [S2](#s2)

Portability should encompass the records a successor needs to understand an existing service, including the meaning of exported data, relevant history and documented dependencies. A file export provides one component of that continuity. Responsibility for interpreting it, preserving its context and supporting a transition completes the commercial picture. SCHISM treats interface access, data access and transfer of support as related commitments that deserve individual description and pricing. The value of an interface depends partly on whether a successor can understand its documented meaning and sustain the surrounding obligations after the original relationship ends.

## 08. Existing-asset economics

An acquired asset retains value through supportable use.

Existing assets carry acquisition history, maintenance obligations and remaining utility. Decisions about extending their useful life should account for supportability, information quality and the cost of future changes. Purchase price alone supplies an incomplete basis for that decision, because inexpensive acquisition can coexist with substantial costs for integration, documentation or continuing support.

The economic case improves when the owner can identify which capabilities and responsibilities remain dependable, which commitments require renewal and which dependencies constrain continued use. Reuse, replacement and retirement can each be sensible outcomes under different facts. This dossier supplies no measured savings, conversion prices or payback period. Its contribution is the decision structure: account for the full support relationship and preserve the evidence needed to revisit the choice as circumstances change.

## 09. The scope of contracted rights

State the requested authority and establish the terms that provide it.

A procurement specification can request rights to retain documentation, access relevant records, obtain continuing support and arrange a successor relationship. The request becomes a usable commitment through the applicable agreement and its defined scope. Commercial ownership, licensing, confidentiality and customer access can create different boundaries for different materials. A supplier can retain valuable intellectual property while agreeing to specified continuity obligations. The commercial task is to establish which obligations justify the price and which residual dependencies the purchaser accepts.

The negotiating objective is precision about the authorized party, permitted purpose, duration, triggering conditions and responsibilities at termination. Relevant counsel and contracting authorities determine the legal effect of the language. SCHISM proposes subjects for negotiation; it assigns no automatic entitlement to supplier intellectual property, source material or security credentials. It also makes no accreditation determination about Darkhive or another supplier. Those conclusions require the actual instruments, applicable authority and product-specific evidence.

Preserve what was recognized, what happened later and what remains unestablished.

That later event supports the importance of industrial continuity, while leaving the dossier's software-control proposition to be assessed on its own evidence.
