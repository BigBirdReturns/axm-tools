Held behind its own authority gates

Every case carries the same boundaries

Five rules bound the work.

01

Visibility grants no authority.

Seeing evidence or preparing a packet grants no entry, spend, work, publication, representation, eligibility, or release authority.

02

Help cannot become a penalty.

Evidence gathered to help cannot silently become insurance, enforcement, eligibility, property, resident, or punitive scoring.

03

People retain authorship.

Affected participants retain correction, refusal, narrowing, deferral, acceptance, and appeal where applicable.

04

Vendors perform jobs. They do not own the model.

No vendor becomes the official record by convenience.

05

Continuity survives the original person.

Offers, decisions, sources, holds, and handoffs must remain replayable after the founder, volunteer, operator, or builder leaves.

What a pilot actually requires

Five decisions make one bounded pilot possible.

A pilot begins when one problem has an accountable sponsor, a funded operator, a defined venue and resources, an authority boundary, and a stop condition.

  1. 01

    Choose one problem.

    Name one specific case the pilot will handle.

    Scope
  2. 02

    Name the accountable sponsor.

    Name the person who owns the public promise, budget, boundaries, and stop authority.

    Sponsor
  3. 03

    Assign and fund the continuity operator.

    Name the person responsible for keeping the case moving and fund the time required. The role cannot default to whoever remembers the context.

    Operator
  4. 04

    Name venue, resources, and field partners.

    What is actually available for the pilot, including lawful access, equipment, skills, money, and escalation paths.

    Venue + resources
  5. 05

    Set the effect boundary and stop conditions.

    What may happen, what may never happen, what requires separate consent or review, and what makes the pilot stop.

    Safety limits
