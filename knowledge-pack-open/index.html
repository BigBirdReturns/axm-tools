An experiment in knowledge portability

You can send a ZIP. Why can't you transfer what you learned?

Files move between applications every day. Decisions, evidence, revisions, and the reasons behind them often stay trapped in a conversation or proprietary workspace. AXM asks a concrete question: can that completed work become a file you own and another program can verify?

For independent implementers

The test is public. So are the failures.

Reproducibility only establishes a foundation. The real test of interoperability is somebody else writing a reader or applying the pack in their own tool. The public protocol draft, reference implementations, manifest, and negative vectors give that person an actual starting point.

The actual proposition

Keep the work. Change the tool.

A retained record should continue to make sense after the model, interface, or vendor changes. AXM Genesis fixes the source bytes, claim relationships, manifest identity and hybrid signatures in a verifiable artifact. A reader can question the interpretation while independently checking what the artifact actually contains.

This preview proves portability and source-bound integrity for a bounded demonstration. It does not certify that a claim is true, that extraction captured everything, or that another assistant will successfully resume an arbitrary task. Those are separate tests and opportunities for independent work.

The open invitation: implement a reader in another language, reproduce a failed case, point out a specification ambiguity, or use the artifact from an application that did not create it. Publish the exact receipt, including failures, on the interoperability challenge.
